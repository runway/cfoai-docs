title: "Use different formulas for Actuals and Forecast"
sidebarTitle: "Actuals and Forecast formulas"
description: "Give a Variable one formula for closed months (Actuals) and another for later months (Forecast), such as imported payroll before Last close and headcount times salary after it."
---

Use separate formulas when closed months should show what your books recorded and later months should show a plan. For example, Payroll can read QuickBooks through **Last close** and calculate Headcount times Average Salary after it. Every workspace has two formula ranges: Actuals, the months through Last close, and Forecast, the months after it. A Variable can have a formula for each. To understand the boundary, read [Actuals, Forecast, and Last close](/concepts/actuals-and-forecast). To move it, see [Set Last close and date defaults](/guides/set-dates-and-last-close).

A formula range needs the Model's base periods: months, unless your Model uses another grain. A formula you save on a row of a table with those periods already covers them, and a row on a table with no periods has one formula for every period. Write range formulas on a table at the base grain. In a monthly Model, a formula written on a table by year is saved at year grain and fills only the year cells; the month cells do not read it.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Ask Ari">
    Name the Variable and both calculations:

    > Use imported Payroll for Actuals and Headcount times Average Salary for Forecast.

    To change only one range, name it:

    > Forecast Software Spend as 4% of Revenue. Leave Actuals as they are.

    Ari saves each formula for its range and tells you if a Variable cannot take a range-specific formula, for example because the table it sits on has no months. A range formula Ari saves on a Variable outside any table covers the Model's base periods, usually months.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="By hand">
    <Steps>
      <Step title="Open the row's formula">
        On a table with months, point at the row name and select the **fx** button, **Edit variable formula**. The editor shows one section for each range, **Actuals** and **Forecast**.

        You can also double-click the row's cell in the **Actuals Formula** or **Forecast Formula** column. The editor opens with that section expanded.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Open the section you want to change">
        Select a section's name to expand or collapse it.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Type the formula for that range">
        For the example, leave **Actuals** reading the imported values and type this in **Forecast**:

        ```text
        Headcount * `Average Salary`
        ```

        Each section reads what you type the way a cell does: a leading `=` marks a formula, `1,000` saves as the number 1000, and plain words in a text Variable save as text.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Save">
        Press **Enter**.
      </Step>
    </Steps>
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

Check the first Forecast month after you save. A formula that reads the previous month, such as `` `Cash Balance`[-1] + `Net Cash Flow` ``, starts from the last Actuals month, so that month needs a value.

## When a section shows a dimmed formula

A section with no formula of its own shows the formula it inherits, dimmed. Point at it to see the source: **Inherited from Actuals formula** (or **Forecast**), and the address that owns it.

- Select **Edit source formula** to open the formula it inherits and change it there. Saving it updates that formula instead of creating a second copy.
- Click the dimmed formula, press **→**, or press **Tab** to copy it into this section and change it for this range only.
- Start typing to replace it.

A formula the section owns opens at full strength and edits in place.

## Show the Actuals and Forecast formulas side by side

On a table with months, the formula column splits into **Actuals Formula** and **Forecast Formula**. To show the formula columns, see [Show or hide the formula columns](/guides/write-a-formula#show-or-hide-the-formula-columns).

- To hide one, right-click its header and choose **Hide column**.
- To bring it back, right-click the other one's header and choose **Show Actuals column** or **Show Forecast column**.
- **Hide all formula columns** hides both.

A row that cannot split by range, such as a row with no months, shows its one formula in the first formula column shown.
