How do 300 accounts in your books become 12 lines on your P&L? A **Dimension mapping** assigns each item of one Dimension to an item of another. Map account 5001 to COGS and account 6001 to Payroll, and every table that reports by the mapped Dimension groups those accounts' amounts under COGS and Payroll. People also call this grouping accounts into categories, or mapping a chart of accounts.

## A mapping groups items into categories

A mapping is a lookup from a source Dimension, such as Account, to a target Dimension, such as Reporting Line:

| Account (source) | Reporting Line (target) |
| --- | --- |
| 4000 Subscription revenue | Revenue |
| 5001 Hosting | COGS |
| 6001 Salaries | Payroll |
| 6002 Payroll taxes | Payroll |

The classification lives in one place. Change account 6002 to another line in the mapping, and every table, formula, and chart that reads Reporting Line follows. Statement lines read their accounts through a mapping this way, so a new account in your books needs one new mapping row, not a new formula.

A mapping does the same job for any set of labels that should report together: two systems' department names into one list, or each employee into a department.

## Mappings go one way

A mapping always runs from source to target. Pick the shared reporting Dimension as the target, and map each source into it. If two companies' accounts must land in one reporting structure, map each company's Account Dimension into the same target. A mapping that would lead back to where it started is refused.

## What a mapping can say

- An assignment applies in every period. Mappings have no effective dates.
- An assignment names a fixed target item. A row of the mapping can instead carry a formula that calculates the mapped value for each record the row matches. That formula does not define the target Dimension's items, and Ari reports such a row as a formula, not as an item.
- One assignment can match several items at once with a condition, such as "8001 or 8002 maps to Other Income."
- An assignment can match a pair of items, such as an account together with a company, so two companies' account 6000 can land on different lines.
- A condition tests the source items only. It cannot test a Variable's value, so a list of exceptions that depends on an amount stays a list of items.
- One target can be fed by a one-key lookup and a two-key lookup, such as Account alone and Account with Account ID. A row that the two-key lookup has no assignment for falls back to the one-key assignment.
- Two lookups that match the same imported rows, where neither includes all of the other's keys, such as one keyed by Team and one keyed by Class, give the target no single answer, and the calculation refuses it. Both can feed the target when a lookup keyed by Team and Class together also applies to those rows. Ari refuses a mapping change, including a removal, that would leave the target, or a Dimension that reads it, unable to calculate.

A Dimension whose items come from a formula cannot also be a mapping target. A Dimension whose items were typed in by hand, with no mapping yet, does not take its first mapping either: mapped membership follows the input records, so the typed list would stop standing on its own. The exception is a mapping column on a table: a cell there accepts an item that is already in the typed list, whether you pick it in a saved table or Ari fills the column while it builds the table, and the typed items and the values saved on them stay. Totals and breakdowns by that Dimension then leave out a typed item that no record maps to, until one does; Ari names those items when its change saves the mapping. A mapping whose target is a separate Dimension leaves the typed list unchanged. Clearing the typed items first deletes the values saved on them. A Dimension that already has a mapping, an empty one, or one fed by a connected source takes a mapping as before.

## The catch-all covers everything else

Each target can have one **catch-all**: the item that every unmatched source item maps to, such as Other. Without a catch-all, an unmatched item stays unmapped, and its cell in a mapping column is blank, so you can see what is left to classify. A catch-all describes the lookups beside it, so when Ari removes a mapping's last lookup, the catch-all goes with it; a catch-all with no lookup would describe no records. Removing one lookup while another remains keeps it.

## How mapped items show in tables

A table can show a mapping in two ways:

- As a mapping column beside each source item, such as accounts on rows and Reporting Line as a column. Each cell shows that row's assignment, and picking another item there changes the mapping for every table.
- As a breakdown that nests the target over the source, such as Department, then Role. A Role the mapping assigns shows once, under its Department, so subtotals count it once. A Role that comes from a source table and that no assignment matches and no catch-all covers forms no row, and neither does a Department that no Role maps to. A Role someone added by hand that no assignment matches shows under every Department the table has, so totals over this breakdown count its values once per Department.

A breakdown can list an item that it does not show yet, because no assignment gives the item a row. Ari can still save a value or a formula for that item. Ari's result tells it that no cell shows the value yet and why, so it can tell you; the row and its value appear once an assignment maps a record to the item.

When Ari creates a Dimension for a mapping, such as a new P&L Line, it gives the Dimension its own table on the **Model** Page, named after the Dimension followed by **mapping**, with the source items down the rows and the new Dimension as a column. That table is where its assignments are read and changed; like any Dimension, it can be shown on other tables too. A Dimension created without a mapping sits on the Model Page's shared **Dimensions** table instead. The Model Page stays the home for new mapping tables after you move it under another Page or rename it. If it is deleted, or who can see it changes, the next mapping Ari creates makes one replacement Page named **Model**, and later mapping tables join that replacement. The replacement sits under the deleted Page's parent only when that parent is still there, has no audience of its own for a subpage to inherit, and you can add Pages under it; otherwise it is a top-level Page.

A mapping column belongs beside the Dimension it is mapped from. An assignment applies only on a row that carries every Dimension its lookup keys on: a lookup keyed by Account alone needs the account, and one keyed by Account and Account ID needs both. So a mapping column beside a different Dimension's rows, such as Reporting Line beside departments, reads the catch-all on every row, or stays blank when the mapping has none. Ari warns when it reads or builds a table laid out that way.

Renaming an item, on a table or through Ari, also renames it in the mapping and in formulas that compare against it, as one change that one undo reverses.

## When a forecast is entered by a broader group than the report's category

A report can group a number by a category that a mapping assigns to each detailed item, while the forecast is entered by a broader group of the same items. Say revenue reports by Class, and a mapping gives each Product its Class: Hardware, Software, or Services. The forecast is entered by product Family, and a second mapping gives each Product its Family. A forecast formula saved for the Core family names no Product, so it carries no Class. When Core's Products all share one Class, nothing is unclear. When Core holds a Hardware Product and a Software Product, nothing saved says how Core's forecast divides between Hardware and Software.

When Ari reads a table like this, or builds or reshapes one, the result tells it where the category comes from and names each group whose items belong to more than one category with no split saved. Ari can then tell you which groups need a split and ask how each one divides. Ari's modeling instructions tell it to keep the amounts as saved until you answer, so it does not divide a group's forecast on its own. The table on the Page shows no mark.

This check reads only saved mappings and saved formulas, so it has limits:

- It covers mappings keyed by one Dimension, where each row maps one item to one fixed item.
- A group stays on the list while the Variable has any formula or typed value saved for that group that names neither the category nor the detailed item. A split saved for some months leaves the group listed for the others, and a split saved another way, such as a separate percentage Variable, still reads as no split.
- A group is left off when two mapping rows of equal rank give one of its items different results, and on a table that compares Scenarios.
- Ari gets these facts only when you can read the category, the detailed Dimension, the group's Dimension, and the Variable.

## Shared Dimensions across accounting connections

When a connection's accounting statements load for the first time, each category column, such as **Entity**, **Currency**, or **Vendor**, joins an existing Dimension that matches it. A match has the same accounting system, column name, type, and aggregation. If several match, it joins the one that the most sources already feed, and the older one when two are tied. So two QuickBooks Online companies share one Entity Dimension and one Currency Dimension, and a Model can report on each company and on the group.

A few limits apply:

- Joining happens once, when a statement first loads. Later syncs never regroup, so a Dimension that stayed separate stays separate.
- Dates and amounts are never combined.
- A QuickBooks Online or Xero company with the same name as one already connected does not join the existing Entity Dimension: it gets an Entity Dimension of its own, so the two cannot share one Entity breakdown. To keep them together, rename one company in its accounting system before connecting it; see [Model several entities and consolidate them](/guides/consolidate-entities).
- Different accounting systems, such as one QuickBooks Online company and one Xero organisation, get separate Dimensions.

Ari can combine compatible Dimensions into one, or split a combined one apart:

- Combining joins Dimensions with matching types and aggregation, keeps each item's exact label, and rewrites the formulas and tables that read the others. An item you added by hand moves to the Dimension that remains, and joins the same item there when one exists. Amounts are never combined, and nothing is saved if the combine is refused. Ari refuses a combine while something saved in the Scenario it is working in names the Dimensions in a way the combine cannot rewrite, such as one hand-added item that names two of them, and tells you what to remove first. An edit saved in another Scenario does not refuse a combine in Main: each Scenario built on Main follows in the same change, and a Snapshot stays as it was. Canvas blocks are not rewritten. Ari gets a list of the Canvas blocks that mention a removed Dimension, so it can repair them. It also gets a list of the blocks with a button that sends Ari a request. The combine could not check what such a request names, so Ari inspects those blocks itself. A chart that quotes a name also present in the kept Dimension continues to work and is not listed.
- Splitting turns a combined Dimension back into one Dimension per source column. Formulas that read amounts stay as they are, so a breakdown may no longer add up to its total, and Ari says so.
