---
title: "Variables and Dimensions"
---

cfo.ai models are built from two kinds of things: **Variables** and **Dimensions**. They are separate concepts, not one thing wearing two hats.

A **Variable** is a named model quantity — a value you calculate, aggregate, or hold, such as Revenue, Headcount, Gross Margin, or Price.

A **Dimension** is a named category you use to group, filter, or organize Variables, such as Department, Region, Product, or Date.

<Note>
  **The same business field can show up as a Dimension in one context and as a mapped value elsewhere**

  Department is usually a Dimension — it's how you break Payroll or Revenue out by team. But "which department does this employee belong to" is itself a value you might calculate: an Employee Variable that returns a Department DimensionItem for each employee. This is called a mapped Dimension.

  - **Department:** As a Dimension, it breaks payroll and operating expenses out by department. As a mapped value on an Employee Variable, it identifies which department each employee belongs to.
  - **Customer segment:** As a Dimension, it breaks revenue out by segment. As a mapped value on a Customer Variable, it identifies each customer's segment, such as Enterprise or SMB.
  - **Account category:** As a Dimension, it structures the P&L into groups such as Revenue, COGS, and OpEx. As a mapped value on an Account Variable, it identifies each GL account's reporting category.

  This lets you define a business concept once and reuse it in different modeling contexts, instead of maintaining disconnected copies of the same data.
</Note>

## Variables answer "how much," Dimensions answer "by what"

A Variable answers questions such as:

- How much revenue did we earn?
- How many employees do we have?
- What is our gross margin?

A Dimension answers questions such as:

- Revenue by which department?
- Headcount in which region?
- Gross margin for which product?

For example, **Revenue by Department** combines both: Revenue is a Variable, and Department is a Dimension used to break it down.

## Variables in a financial model

A Variable can represent:

- A financial line item, such as Revenue or Cost of Goods Sold
- A KPI or operating driver, such as Headcount or Units Sold
- An assumption, such as Price or Commission Rate
- A calculated output, such as Gross Profit or Gross Margin
- A date used to place values in time

A Dimension, by contrast, represents a category, such as Department, Entity, Product, or Region.

## Data type does not decide which one something is

Numbers aren't always Variables. Employee ID and Account Code may contain numbers, but they identify categories and are usually Dimensions.

Text isn't always a Dimension. A calculated Variable can return a label, even though most calculated financial outputs are numeric.

Decide based on how the field is used in the model: is it the value you're measuring, or the category you're organizing by?

## How formulas fit

A formula calculates a Variable. Dimensions backed by source data can also use formulas to determine their values through a mapping, but Dimension formulas have narrower behavior than Variable formulas.
