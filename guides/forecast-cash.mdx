title: "Forecast cash and cash runway"
sidebarTitle: "Forecast cash"
description: "Have Ari forecast your monthly cash balance and cash runway, from the cash on your balance sheet plus forecast inflows and outflows, add a 13-week weekly view, and test a raise or a spending cut in a Scenario."
---

Ask Ari for a monthly cash forecast to see how long your cash lasts and what would change that. The forecast names the month cash falls below your minimum or runs out, and you can test the decisions that move that month. People also call this a burn forecast or a cash runway model.

Before you start, connect your accounting system so the forecast starts from the cash on your balance sheet; see [Connect QuickBooks, Xero, NetSuite, or Rillet](/guides/connect-accounting). A revenue and expense forecast gives Ari the money coming in and going out; see [Build a revenue and expense forecast](/guides/build-a-forecast). Without either, tell Ari your cash balance and your main costs. Ari saves them as inputs you can edit, and its reply names which numbers are its estimates and what each rests on.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Ask for a monthly cash forecast">
    Say how far ahead to look and what cash level matters to you:

    > Forecast monthly cash for the next 18 months and show when cash falls below six months of operating expenses.

    Ari starts from the cash balance on your balance sheet, as of its date, then adds the money coming in and subtracts the money going out, month by month. It names the first month the balance falls below your minimum, or below zero if you ask when cash runs out. If cash never falls that low within the forecast, Ari says it lasts through the last forecast month.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check where the cash comes from">
    > Where does the starting cash balance come from, and which inflows and outflows move the forecast the most?

    The starting balance comes from the cash accounts on your synced balance sheet. When Ari has no balance to read, it uses a starting balance you can edit, and it tells you the cash runway depends on that number.

    Cash moves when money is paid, which can be a different month from when it is earned. Ask Ari to use your real payment terms, such as customers paying 30 days after the invoice or annual contracts paid up front. A balance that goes negative stays visible in the forecast, and money you have actually raised stays separate from a raise you are only considering.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add a 13-week view, if you need one">
    > Build a 13-week cash forecast by week, starting next week.

    Ari builds a weekly table and writes formulas for weeks, because formulas written for months do not fill week columns. Your accounting statements arrive one month at a time. Weekly actuals need a source with a date on every transaction, such as a bank export; see [Upload a CSV as a data source](/guides/upload-a-csv).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Test a raise or a cut in a Scenario">
    Try each decision in its own Scenario, so Main stays as it is:

    > Create a Scenario called Bridge round: raise \$2 million in March, and compare cash runway with Main.

    > Create a Scenario where we cut marketing spend by 30% starting next month, and show the month cash runs out.

    See [Test a decision in a Scenario](/guides/test-a-decision-in-a-scenario) and [Compare Scenarios side by side](/guides/compare-scenarios).
  </Step>
</Steps>

Your cash actuals move forward when Last close moves past a month; see [Actuals, Forecast, and Last close](/concepts/actuals-and-forecast). To get a cash summary on a schedule, see [Schedule a recurring report with a Trigger](/guides/schedule-a-trigger).
