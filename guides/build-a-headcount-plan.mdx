---
title: "Build a headcount plan"
description: "Turn your HR system's employee records, or an uploaded roster, into a roster and a monthly headcount and payroll table, then add planned hires and see what they cost."
---

Ask Ari for a headcount plan to see who is employed each month, what they cost, and what planned hires will add. Ari builds a headcount Page from your employee records, with the formulas behind every number.

Before you start, connect your HR or payroll system, such as Rippling, or upload an employee roster as a CSV; see [Connect payroll, HR, CRM, payments, or a database](/guides/connect-other-sources) or [Upload a CSV as a data source](/guides/upload-a-csv). Then set Last close to your last closed month; see [Set Last close and date defaults](/guides/set-dates-and-last-close). Months through Last close show what your records held. Later months carry each person's latest record forward and add your planned hires.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Ask Ari for the plan">
    > Build a headcount plan by department from our Rippling data, with loaded payroll cost.

    Ari reads the connection's fields and finds what the plan needs:

    - Employee ID, which tells people apart. Two people with the same name stay two people.
    - Start date and termination date, which decide who is employed in each month. An empty termination date means the person is still employed.
    - Pay and how it is paid: annual, monthly, or hourly. Hourly pay needs an hours assumption, which Ari keeps as an input you can edit.
    - Department, which groups people on the monthly table.

    Job title, employment type, location, pay currency, and other fields come along when the source has them. Name the Page you want, or accept Ari's name for it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the two tables">
    The Page has two tables built from the same records:

    - A roster, with one row per employee, keyed by Employee ID, and each field in its own column. It shows each person's latest values. To show each person's name as the row name instead of the ID, see [Show a field, such as Name, as the row names](/guides/format-a-table#show-a-field-such-as-name-as-the-row-names).
    - A monthly table of headcount and payroll cost, grouped by department. Each month reads each person's latest record dated in that month. A month with no record yet, such as a future month, carries the last one forward.

    A connected HR system saves each sync's values as a dated record, so a raise or a transfer made after you connect shows in the month it happened. The records start with the first sync; cfo.ai does not load earlier pay or department history from the HR system. A one-time roster upload has no history at all. In both cases Ari can apply each person's current pay and department to earlier months, and it tells you that this is an assumption, not recorded payroll. Start and termination dates still decide which months count each person.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check how Ari counts and pays">
    Ari follows these conventions unless you tell it otherwise:

    - A month's headcount counts everyone employed at any point in the month, so the start month and the termination month both count. If you report month-end headcount, say so.
    - Annual pay divided by twelve is a full month's pay, and a monthly rate is a month's pay as it stands. A start or termination month is paid for the days employed in it.
    - Loaded cost, such as employer taxes and benefits, comes from your records when they hold it. The employee records an HR connection brings in hold pay, target bonus, and on-target commission, and no employer taxes or benefits. So Ari usually adds a loaded-cost assumption as an input you can change, and names it in its reply.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add planned hires">
    > Add three sales hires starting in March at \$120,000 a year each.

    Ari adds the planned hires as rows you can edit, each with a department, start date, and pay. A row holds one hire, or several hires that start together on the same pay, with a headcount, such as one Sales row that counts three. If you want one row per person, say so. When you move a start date, headcount and payroll move with it. A planned hire is a row in your Model, not a record from your HR system, so a sync does not change it. When a planned hire starts and appears in your records, ask Ari to take that hire out of the plan so nobody is counted twice: lower the planned row's headcount by one, or remove the row when it holds only that hire. Ari also looks for this before it reports payroll. When a planned hire has the same name as someone in your records, or the same role and start month, Ari names the pair and each one's monthly pay before it gives a payroll total, and keeps both until you say which to keep. Two different people with the same role and start month can be named this way too, so say when they are not the same person. Ari adds the hires to the Scenario you are working in and names it in its reply. To try a hiring plan without changing your main plan, ask for it in a Scenario:

    > Create a Scenario with three more sales hires and show how they affect payroll and cash over the next two quarters.
  </Step>
</Steps>

If you have no roster at all, tell Ari how many people you employ, in which departments, and roughly what they cost. Ari creates a row for each person, named by role and numbered, such as Software Engineer 2, or one row with a headcount for a group of people in the same role. Each row's pay is a rate for its role, and Ari reconciles the rows to the totals you gave.
