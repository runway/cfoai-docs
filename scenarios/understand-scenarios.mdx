A Scenario lets you explore a different version of your Model without changing Main. Use one when a pricing decision, hiring plan, financing event, or other assumption needs its own saved alternative.

## Main and child Scenarios

Every Model has one **Main** Scenario. Main holds the numbers your team treats as the current plan, and it's what most Pages show by default.

An editable Scenario branches from Main. Changes you make in that Scenario, such as updating a formula or adjusting a headcount assumption, apply there until you decide whether to merge them into Main.

Child Scenarios are useful for:

- Modeling a what-if question, like a hiring freeze or a pricing change, without disturbing the plan everyone else is working from.
- Drafting next quarter's Forecast before you're ready to share it.
- Comparing two versions of the same plan side by side.

## How a Scenario stays current

An editable Scenario automatically inherits updates from Main while keeping its own changes. For example, refreshed accounting data can appear in both Main and your hiring-plan Scenario, while the alternative hiring assumption remains specific to the Scenario.

Connected source data is shared, except with a Snapshot, which stops receiving integration updates. A Scenario changes the Model rules, values, and presentation that differ from Main; it does not create a private copy of an integration.

A Scenario stores only the values that differ from the Scenario it branched from. If you ask Ari to set a value that the Scenario already inherits, nothing is saved: Ari reports that the cell keeps following Main and changes when Main changes. Give the Scenario a different value when you want it to hold still while Main moves.

## What a Scenario would merge in

Before merging a Scenario, ask Ari what would change. Ari can inspect the saved change summary and explain which Variables, formulas, values, Pages, or tables differ from Main.

Merging applies the Scenario's changes to Main, the Scenario it branched from, and removes the merged Scenario. Review the affected changes before approving the merge. Undoing the merge from the version history brings the merged Scenario back.

A Scenario that has an editable child Scenario of its own cannot be merged. The refusal reads **scenario cannot be merged while it has 1 child scenario** and names the child; merge or delete that child first. A Snapshot taken from the Scenario does not block the merge: after its source Scenario is merged away, the Snapshot stays readable at the version it was pinned to, with the edits typed into it, and undo or redo of the merge keeps it readable too.

## Snapshots preserve a point in time

A **Snapshot** pins Main or an editable Scenario to a specific point in its history. After that point, nothing from the source flows into it:

- **Integration updates.** When QuickBooks or another source syncs, Main updates and the Snapshot keeps the numbers it had.
- **Edits to the source.** A formula, assumption, or hard-coded value someone changes in Main later stays out of the Snapshot.
- **Table setup.** Later changes to a Table Block's settings or column widths in the source do not carry over.

Use a Snapshot to preserve a submitted budget, board-reporting baseline, or the plan your team approved at a particular date.

To create one, ask Ari and name the Scenario to preserve, or take it yourself: open the Scenario picker while viewing the Scenario and select **Take snapshot**, or open **Version history** and choose **Take snapshot here** on the entry to pin. The **Take snapshot** dialog suggests the name **\[Scenario] snapshot** and saves with **Create snapshot**. Taking a Snapshot yourself needs permission to create Scenarios, and neither control is offered on a phone or while you are viewing a Snapshot.

A Snapshot also has these limits:

- **A Snapshot is not read-only.** You and Ari can still type hard-coded values over its numbers, and those edits save. Treat a Snapshot as a copy your team agrees not to touch.
- **A Snapshot cannot be merged** into Main.
- **A Snapshot cannot be reverted** to an earlier point, and you cannot take a Snapshot of another Snapshot. To pin an earlier point of a Scenario, use **Take snapshot here** on that entry in the Scenario's version history.

## Review and reverse changes

cfo.ai keeps a version history of every write to a Scenario. Open it with the **Version history** control in the Page header. Each entry's actions menu offers **Copy link to version** and these ways to reverse a change:

- **Undo this change** for a specific change or a related batch of changes. An entry the Scenario inherited from Main cannot be undone from the child.
- **Redo this change** on an entry you undid.
- **Restore this version** to return the Scenario to an earlier point in its history. This rolls back every later change, so review the impact first. The restore itself becomes a history entry you can undo. Ari calls the same action a revert.

Ask Ari to show you the history, or to explain what changed between two points in time, before deciding to undo or restore.
