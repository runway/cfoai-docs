---
title: "Review and troubleshoot Ari's formula changes"
description: "Review the target, expression, scope, formula range, Scenario, and actual calculated values after Ari changes a formula."
---

After Ari writes a formula, review the formula card and the calculated result.

## Review the formula card

In the chat, Ari's formula writes collect under a **Formulas** card. Expand a write to see one card per formula it saved, showing the Variable's name, the condition that says where the rule applies, the expression, and the formula range when the rule is limited to one. Check:

- **Variable:** Is this the Variable you meant to change?
- **Expression:** Does it describe the intended business rule?
- **Scope:** Does the condition apply everywhere, to a Dimension, or to a specific segment?
- **Formula range:** Does it apply to Actuals, Forecast, or both? A card with no range name applies to every period.
- **Scenario:** The card does not name it. The rule lands in the Scenario the conversation is in unless you named another, so check the Scenario shown in the sidebar.

## Ask Ari to verify the result

> Check the resulting values in the P&L table and compare them with Revenue minus Cost of Goods Sold.

> Verify this formula for Sales and Engineering for the next three months.

A successful save confirms that the formula is valid. Checking calculated values confirms that its business behavior is also correct.

## Diagnose before changing

If a result is unexpected, ask Ari to inspect the current formulas first:

> Explain why this formula works for Sales but not Marketing. Do not change anything yet.

> Check whether a more specific formula is overriding this one.

> Check whether this lookup returns several values and needs an aggregation.

> Explain why this formula cannot be assigned only to Forecast.

## Correct a formula

Once the cause is clear, describe the intended correction and scope. Ask Ari to validate, write, and verify the revised formula.

For several changes at once, ask which items were applied and whether any were rejected. Confirm the final values instead of assuming that a successful-looking response proves the financial result is correct.
