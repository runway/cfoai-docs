Your first Model is a starting point. Over the next five days, we turn it into something you use every week: a forecast you can explain, a Page you can share, a decision you can test before you make it, and a weekly update you do not have to ask for. Each lesson takes about ten minutes.

Before you start, finish [Build your first Model](/tutorials/first-model). Each lesson begins in a new chat: press **⌘ /** (**Ctrl /** on Windows and Linux). Replace the words in brackets with your own.

## Day 2: Build out your forecast

Today we make the forecast explain itself, so you can say which assumptions move it.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Ask Ari to build from what drives your business">
    > Build out our forecast. Calculate revenue from [new customers, pricing, and churn] and costs from [headcount and our biggest vendors]. Put the assumptions where I can see and change them.

    Notice the assumptions Ari adds, such as a churn rate or a price. They sit in the Model where you can see and change them.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Trace one forecast number">
    Right-click a revenue cell in a future month, choose **Mention cell in chat**, and ask:

    > How is this calculated? Which assumptions move it the most?

    Notice that Ari shows the formula behind the number and names the assumptions it reads.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Have Ari explain a whole table">
    Point at the left edge of a table, select its six-dot handle, and choose **Explain with Ari**.

    Notice that a new chat opens with a request to explain the table. Ari answers with what the table shows, how it is built, and how to read it.
  </Step>
</Steps>

You are done when you can name the two or three assumptions that move your forecast most.

## Day 3: Build a Page you would share

Today we turn the Model into a Page other people can read, and create a link to share it.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Ask for a summary Page">
    > Build a [board / monthly] summary Page with a row of our five key numbers and their change since last month, a cash runway gauge, and a slider so I can try different hiring dates and see the effect on cash.

    The gauge and the slider are Canvas blocks: custom visuals Ari writes for you from live Model data. Move the slider and notice that the chart changes but your saved assumptions do not. A slider previews; it never changes the Model.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Browse the Canvas library for ideas">
    Select **Browse Canvas block library**, the palette icon at the bottom left of the message box, pick a template that looks useful, and select **Add (1)**. The template joins your message as a chip. Send the message, and Ari builds it.

    <Frame caption="The Canvas block library button sits at the bottom left of the chat message box.">
      <img src="/images/canvas-block-library-button.png" width="367" alt="The Ari chat message box with the palette icon at the bottom left highlighted and labeled Browse Canvas block library." />
    </Frame>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Share the Page with a read-only link">
    Select **Share** in the Page header. Under **External access**, select **Create link**, then select the copy button on the new link, **Copy external link**.

    Paste the link into a private or incognito browser window. Notice that the Page opens without a sign-in, and that you can read it but not change it. Anyone you send the link to sees the same.
  </Step>
</Steps>

You are done when your Page opens through its link.

## Day 4: Test a decision in a Scenario

Today we see what a decision costs before we make it, without changing your plan.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Ask Ari for a Scenario">
    > Create a Scenario called [Hire two engineers in Q1]. [Add two engineers at \$X per year starting in January.] Compare cash and cash runway with Main, and add the comparison to my [board] Page.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Look at both versions">
    Notice the Scenario picker in the sidebar, above your Pages. Main is your plan; the new Scenario holds only the hires. On the Page, the comparison shows both versions of cash side by side.
  </Step>
</Steps>

You are done when your Page shows what the decision does to cash runway. To learn how Scenarios stay in step with Main, read [Scenarios and Snapshots](/concepts/scenarios).

## Day 5: Pressure-test your Model

Every company is different, so today we ask Ari where your Model is weakest.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Ask for a review">
    > Review my Model without changing it. Which assumptions look weakest compared with our history? What is missing, and what should I build next?

    Because you said not to change anything, Ari reads and reports. Nothing in the Model moves.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fix the most important gap">
    Pick the suggestion that matters most, such as checking a growth rate against your Actuals or [building headcount from payroll](/guides/build-a-headcount-plan), and ask Ari to do it.

    Notice that the Pages that show the change update as Ari saves it.
  </Step>
</Steps>

You are done when you have fixed or built one thing Ari flagged.

## Day 6: Let Ari keep watch

Today we set up a weekly update, so you hear what matters without opening the app.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Schedule a Trigger">
    A Trigger runs a prompt on a schedule and sends you the result:

    > Every Monday at 8am [your time zone], summarize last week's cash in and out, show our current cash runway, and flag any expense that looks different from our recent pattern. Include a link to my [board] Page.

    Ari asks whether to send results to your Slack direct messages or your email. Choose one.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check that it is saved">
    > List my Triggers.

    Notice the name and schedule Ari saved.
  </Step>
</Steps>

You are done when you have one Trigger saved.

## What you built

In one week you went from a first draft to a working setup: a forecast you can explain, a Page you can share, a decision tested in a Scenario, a Model you have pressure-tested, and a weekly update that arrives on its own.

## Next steps

- After your books close each month, compare your Actuals with your Forecast and update the assumptions that turned out wrong: [Update your plan after you close the month](/guides/close-the-month).
- Teach Ari a process you repeat, such as your month-end review: [Save a workflow as a Skill](/guides/save-a-skill).
- Look up more prompts in the [Prompt library](/reference/prompt-library).
