Ask Ari for a monthly forecast of revenue and expenses, such as a 12-month operating plan. Ari builds it from what moves your numbers: customers and price, deals in your pipeline, or people and vendors. The forecast lands in your Model as Pages you can open, trace, and edit.

Before you start, connect your accounting system so the forecast starts from your actuals; see [Connect QuickBooks, Xero, NetSuite, or Rillet](/guides/connect-accounting). To forecast revenue from your deals or payroll from your employee records, connect your CRM or payroll system too; see [Connect payroll, HR, CRM, payments, or a database](/guides/connect-other-sources). Then check that Last close is the last month you have closed; see [Set Last close and date defaults](/guides/set-dates-and-last-close). If your business has no data yet, skip to [Plan a new business that has no data yet](#plan-a-new-business-that-has-no-data-yet).

<Steps>
  <Step title="Ask Ari for the forecast">
    Tell Ari what creates revenue, what creates cost, and what decision the forecast is for, and name the time span you want.

    > We sell annual software subscriptions in three plans. Forecast revenue for the next 12 months from customers by plan: new customers each month, churn, and renewals. Forecast expenses from headcount and our five largest vendors. Label every assumption you make.

    Ari starts building without waiting for an approval, because you can undo a change to your Model; see [Undo a change or restore an earlier version](/guides/undo-changes). It builds a new forecast in Main unless you name another Scenario. Pages appear in the sidebar as Ari builds them.

    To check the plan before Ari builds anything, ask for it in the same message:

    > Show me your plan and the assumptions you would use before you build anything.

    Ari then shows a plan card that lists what it will create and the assumptions it intends to use, and waits. Change any assumption that is wrong, and select **Build it**. See [Approve a plan](/guides/approve-ari-actions#approve-a-plan).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Ask for the method that fits each part">
    Say how each part of the business really works, so the forecast follows it:

    | Your revenue comes from | Ask Ari to forecast it from |
    | --- | --- |
    | Subscriptions or ARR | Customers by plan: opening customers, new customers, churn, renewals, and price |
    | A sales pipeline | Your deals: bookings from won deals in the month each closed, and weighted pipeline from open deals, each weighted by its probability in the month it is expected to close. Ask for revenue to follow each contract's start date and terms, so bookings, revenue, and cash collected stay separate lines. |
    | Usage or transactions | Volume times price |
    | Projects or services | Projects or billable people times their rate |
    | A steady history | A run rate or growth rate from your recent actuals |

    Costs work the same way. Payroll follows headcount, hosting follows customers or usage, and a percentage of revenue fits only a cost that really moves with revenue. When you are not sure which method fits, ask Ari to recommend one and to say what the others would change. Ari is instructed to state the method and the key assumptions when it hands over a build; ask for them when a reply leaves them out.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the starting month">
    Ari reads your connected actuals through Last close, and the forecast starts the month after. A forecast that grows from the last actual month repeats anything unusual in it, such as a one-time bill or a large refund. Ask Ari to start from an average instead when that month is not typical.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the assumptions">
    > Which numbers in this forecast are assumptions, and where did each one come from?

    Ari lists each assumption and its source: your books, something you told it, research, or its own estimate. Change any of them by typing the new value in its cell, or by telling Ari.

    To check the forecast against your recent history, ask Ari to compare each cost line, and each KPI that has a target, over the first three forecast months with the last three months of actuals. Ari is instructed to make this comparison before it reports a forecast and to state each step change or gap to target with its months and its source; ask for it when a reply leaves it out. For collections and payment days, see [Forecast cash and cash runway](/guides/forecast-cash).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check that the totals tie">
    > Check that revenue by plan adds up to total revenue, that department payroll matches the employee detail, and that quarterly and yearly totals add up correctly.

    Ari reads the tables, compares the totals, and tells you what ties and what does not. For why a total might not add up, see [Totals, aggregation, and time rollups](/concepts/totals-and-rollups).
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Change the forecast method, such as a 3-month to a 6-month run rate

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Ask Ari">
    Name the Variable and the new method:

    > Change the forecast for Software spend from the average of the last 3 months of actuals to the average of the last 6 months.

    Ari changes the Variable's Forecast formula, and every forecast month recalculates.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="By hand">
    <Steps>
      <Step title="Open the Forecast formula">
        On a table with months, point at the Variable's row name and select the **fx** button, **Edit variable formula**. Expand the **Forecast** section. See [Use different formulas for Actuals and Forecast](/guides/actuals-and-forecast-formulas).
      </Step>
      <Step title="Change the date range">
        In the formula, select the reference, such as Software spend. Its panel opens. Select the date filter that reads **Last 3 months of actuals**, and choose **Last 6 months of actuals**. The other choices are **Last 12 months of actuals**, **Year to date**, **Quarter to date**, and **Custom range**.

        If the date filter reads **Custom range**, the formula uses a range that no preset names, such as the three months before each forecast month. Choosing **Last 6 months of actuals** replaces it with the six months that end at Last close, the same months for every forecast month. See [Date filter presets](/reference/formula-syntax#date-filter-presets).
      </Step>
      <Step title="Save">
        Press **Enter**. Every forecast month recalculates.
      </Step>
    </Steps>
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Plan a new business that has no data yet

With no books to start from, Ari builds the forecast from your description of the product, its price, and its costs. It saves every number it estimates as an input you can change, and its reply names which numbers are estimates and what each rests on.

In a new workspace, Ari's first card asks where you would like to start.

- Choose **My business**, then **I want to type or upload context**, and describe your product, price, costs, team, and cash. Ari builds a complete first plan from your words, saves every number it estimated as an input you can change, and names those estimates in its reply.
- Choose **Something hypothetical** to see an example first. Then choose **A business like mine** for a sample business that resembles yours, labeled as a sample, or **Another business, I'll choose** and type a name. Ari builds a complete model of that business, with the same Pages, records, controls, and Scenarios it would build for your own, and a walkthrough of them, and ends with two or three things to try next.

The card's last question, **What do you want to see first?**, is optional. Choose **P&L and forecast** to have Ari lead with the forecast, then select **Get started**. For every choice on the card, see [Starting card](/reference/chat#starting-card).

In a workspace you already use, describe the business in a chat:

> I'm launching a meal-kit subscription next spring at \$60 a month. I expect 200 subscribers in the first month and 15% growth each month after. Food and shipping cost about \$35 per box. Two founders, no salaries for the first six months, and \$250,000 in the bank. Build a 24-month plan with cash runway, and label every assumption.

Then test prices and hiring plans in Scenarios, so your base plan stays as it is:

> Create a Scenario where the price is \$70 a month and growth is 10%, and compare cash runway with Main.

> Create a Scenario where we hire two engineers in month 6 at \$140,000 a year each, and show the month cash runs out.

See [Test a decision in a Scenario](/guides/test-a-decision-in-a-scenario).
